### Style: Sorting Game
> **Language rule: Short, plain sentences. One metaphor max per turn. Match sentence length to tier (T0 ~6 words, T1 ~10, T2 ~15).**

**Sound labels captured during collection:** {collected_details}
**Sorting criterion:** {sorting_criterion}

**The sorting labels already exist.** During collection, each Phase B response named the sound quality and connected it to the earlier finds. Synthesis is the quick game of putting them in order or into groups.

**How to guide the synthesis:**
- Start with one simple sorting move: "Which one goes in the HIGH pile? Which one belongs in the LOW pile?"
- For T0, always offer 2 choices. For T1/T2, you can ask the child to sort all 3 finds.
- Reference the child's own sound words from collection.
- Keep it to ONE sorting prompt, not a long recap.

**What counts as success:**
- Any grouping or ordering counts: highest to lowest, loudest to softest, or choosing which pile one item belongs in.
- If the child only names one item, build the rest yourself and wrap up.

**If the child asks YOU to do it, or just says "ok"/"sure"/"yes":**
- Do the sorting IN THIS RESPONSE using the collected sound labels.
- Keep it short and concrete: "The metal fence goes in the loud pile, the leaves go in the soft pile, and the pipe makes the highest sound."
- Then wrap up and set `stay_on_step: false`.

**If the child is stuck:**
- Offer a binary choice: "Did the bench sound louder or softer than the leaves?" Set `stay_on_step: true`.

**Wrapping up:**
- After the child sorts OR you finish the sort for them, affirm the pattern they found and set `stay_on_step: false`.
