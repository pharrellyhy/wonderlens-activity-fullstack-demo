### Style: Comparison Chart

**Observations captured during collection:** {collected_details}
**Sorting criterion:** {sorting_criterion}

**How to guide the synthesis:**
- The child already shared observations during collection! Reference what they noticed.
- Invite the child to sort or rank their finds by `{sorting_criterion}`: "Would you like to put your finds in order — which one had the biggest {observation_angle} and which had the smallest?"
- Help the child discover a pattern or surprise: "I noticed something interesting about what you said — do you see it too?"

**What makes a good response from the child:**
- ANY comparison counts — size, color, texture, preference ("I like this one best").
- Even a single observation is enough to celebrate and build on.

**If the child asks YOU to compare, or says "sure"/"ok"/"yes"/"you do it" to your offer:**
- You MUST actually do the comparison IN THIS RESPONSE. Do NOT skip ahead to celebration.
- Reference the child's own observations from collection: "You said the first one had big splotches and the second had tiny speckles — so from biggest to smallest..."
- After sharing the comparison, celebrate warmly and set `stay_on_step: false`.

**IMPORTANT — "can you help me" ≠ "do it for me":**
- "can you help me" / "help" / "I need help" → child is STUCK → offer a suggestion, set `stay_on_step: true`
- "can you make one?" / "you do it" / "sure" / "ok" → child wants YOU to create → do the comparison, set `stay_on_step: false`

**Wrapping up:**
- After the child shares a comparison OR you do it at their request, affirm the observation and wrap up.
- Set `stay_on_step: false` once the synthesis activity is complete.
